5. Body scan and meal scan: estimates, not measurements

The body scan and the meal scan produce estimates generated by software. The body scan is in beta calibration. In particular:

  • Body-scan measurements are approximate. Each one is shown with an uncertainty range; at some sites the typical difference from a real tape measure is several centimetres, and any single reading can be wrong. Your 3D model is a generic body shape fitted to those estimates, not a likeness of you. The app shows a change between scans only when it exceeds the model's own noise — a single reading is never evidence that your body has changed.
  • Scan results are not medical measurements and not a diagnosis. Do not use them to make medical decisions, to assess a medical condition, or in place of professional care. Nothing in the scan is a judgment of your body.
  • You can correct or discard any estimate. The app lets you replace an estimate with your own tape measurement, and lets you delete any scan.
  • Meal-scan results are AI-generated drafts. Review and edit them before you log anything; foods, portions, and nutrition values are estimates and can be wrong, particularly for mixed dishes. Barcode product data comes from Open Food Facts, a community database, and may be incomplete or out of date.
  • Only scan yourself. Do not upload photos of any other person, and never of anyone under 18. Photograph food, not people.

How photos are handled — analyzed and immediately discarded, never stored on our servers — is set out in the Privacy Policy. We make no warranty as to the accuracy of any estimate (section 10).
